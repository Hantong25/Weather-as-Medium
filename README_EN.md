# Accumulus — Concept 3D · 2026.10.27.1

Accumulus is the sky software for the installation EPHEMERAL (named 2026-10-27).

Current concept: a horizontal canopy overhead receives the projection on its underside; its size and position are worked out in the canopy study (the factory default is still A0, 0.841 × 1.189 m). Below sits a 1 × 1 m square base: a black timber frame, two rows of grey pavers around a central black-gravel area (about 0.58 × 0.58 m, estimated from a photo and editable); once filled, only an irregular low patch in its middle holds water (about 0.42 × 0.38 m overall, editable), with dry stones all round. The water level is fixed; water-level interaction is not used. The projected sky is unchanged.

Double-click `Start Demo.command` and keep Terminal open. Python 3 and a modern WebGL 2 browser are required; do not open HTML directly. Use `python3 launch.py --lang en` for English. The top-right version tag should read `CONCEPT 3D · 2026.10.27.1`.

## Workspaces

- **Concept 3D**: a fixed model. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom; arrow keys and + / − also work. Views: overview, visitor view (through the reflection-check visitor's eyes), looking down, build layers, plan. Toggle separated layers, labels and gallery lighting (dark / bright). The build list marks which dimensions are decided and which are estimates or assumptions.
- **Canopy study**: uses plane-mirror reflection to find which part of the ceiling visitors see in the pond. Set the canopy width, depth, underside height and centre offset; where visitors stand (all round, front half, front 90°, front only), the nearest and farthest eye distance from the pond centre, the lowest and highest eye height, and the gravel and water sizes. Only the water reflects the canopy, so coverage counts the water alone. The panel gives the worst and mean share of the water showing sky, the smallest canopy that fills every reflection (one click to apply), and the canopy needed at other heights. In 3D, amber marks ceiling that visitors see but that has no projection, floor dots are coloured by standing spot, and white lines trace the reflection-check visitor's sight lines off four points of the shore to the ceiling. The canopy width and depth also set the aspect of the "Fill" projection.
- **Projection settings**: the sky loops morning → day → sunset glow → starry night, 60 s each by default (240 s per loop). The sun follows its real October path over Melbourne (rising east-south-east, about 59° high in the north at noon, setting west-south-west) and each part takes one stretch of it: morning around sunrise (about −6.6° to +9°), day from mid-morning to mid-afternoon (28°–59°), sunset glow around sunset (+9° to −8°), and night a clear starry sky (sun below −24°) with a full moon opposite the sun lighting one side of the clouds. Sky colour is computed from atmospheric scattering: Rayleigh (blue sky), Mie (bright haze around the sun) and ozone absorption (blue-violet twilight), with automatic exposure. Clouds are lit by sunlight (reddening at low sun, with high cloud lit briefly after sunset), moonlight, skylight and city glow; a low sun lights cloud bases from the side, turning them pink, orange and gold around sunrise and sunset. Under Timing each part can be switched on or off (at least one stays on) and lasts 2–300 s; each change of part has a transition that starts a set number of seconds before the part ends and ends a set number of seconds into the next part (10 s each by default, a sixth of a part), both freely set. When the next part follows on along the sun's path the sun moves on smoothly, never jumping or reversing; when a part in between is off (or only one part is on) the two skies cross-fade instead of racing through the missing part. The quick 60 s, natural 240 s and slow 660 s presets set durations and transitions only, not which parts are on. The night no longer closes into overcast, and the loop no longer plays rain sound by itself (rain plays only in previews). Output is fixed at 24 fps: sky light blends every frame between two precomputed sun positions while the next one is built in the background, so fast dawn and dusk changes stay smooth. Sky combinations: six atmosphere types (Melbourne everyday, clear after rain, soft sea haze, hazy warm sun, bushfire smoke, volcanic twilight [approx.]) change the air itself while colour is still computed by scattering; six colour grades (natural, warm film, cool morning, dreamy pink-violet, soft pastel, black and white) sit on the final image and combine with any atmosphere, and Grade strength tones them down (0: no grade). Style strength tones down the pastel and watercolour strokes and paper. Colour by part sets, for each of the four parts, a sky colour, a colour for the lit side of the clouds (sun or moon) and for their shaded side (skylight and city glow), each with its strength, plus cloud transparency, saturation and star brightness: colours change hue, not brightness, strength 0 keeps the physically computed colour, and during a transition the two parts' settings blend smoothly. Turbidity, night city glow and night moonlight sliders remain. Canopy orientation turns the whole sky — sun, wind and clouds — together: at 0° north is at the top of the picture and east on the left (seen from below); in the gallery, turn it until the projected sky matches the real directions (up to 10.24.5 the sun stayed put when turning, and a separate image north rotation did that; the two are now one). Clouds drift and change shape at constant speeds: the drift and shape-change speeds under Wind and motion stay the same throughout, whatever the weather phase or the live wind speed; Melbourne's live wind only sets the direction, and the clouds turn over about half a minute when it changes. Wisps inside a cloud move at an even pace. While the control window is in the background a Worker keeps time, so the projection window still gets updates at the normal pace. The upper layers of each cloud lean towards their own random direction (up to 10.16.2 they all leant towards the centre of the frame, which looked radial). One cloud per visitor: it fades in on a clear patch of the canopy, drifts with the wind and leaves the picture whole before fading; as it starts to leave, a new cloud with a different shape drifts in from beyond the opposite (upwind) side, with the visitors' clouds staggered so they never travel as one column; it fades out after they leave. In still air (drift speed 0) a cloud dissipates where it is after a minute or two and a new one forms. Cloud appear time (2–60 s, 8 s by default) and Cloud disappear time (2–60 s, 10 s by default) under Audience set how long a visitor's cloud takes to fade in when they arrive and out when they leave; a cloud forming in place takes 1.25 times the appear time and dissipating 1.2 times the disappear time. The defaults are the earlier timing, and presets with the single appear / disappear time of 10.24.5 are converted. Cloud shapes differ on every launch. “Cloud shape and texture” has 13 sliders — shape: cloud size, outline warp, texture scale, billows, depth lean; thickness: density, core shadow; edge: edge detail, edge erosion, edge softness, silver lining, cloud merging, edge bumps — and 4 starting points: Soft · default, Ragged edges (after the irregular crisp edges and grey cores of 10.06.3), Dense cumulus and Thin wisps. Less cloud merging keeps neighbouring clouds apart instead of drawing strings between them (1 is the earlier look); edge bumps give the outline rounded bulges and bays (0 is the earlier look). At their defaults the clouds are those of 10.19.2; the cloud form and the thirteen values can be saved as a cloud look of their own and loaded at any time, are also saved with parameter presets, and follow in the projection window. Cloud region limits where the big clouds form to one part of the image (centre, width and height as shares of the frame), and makes them smaller and closer together to suit it; the sky and its thin background cloud outside stay as they are, so the image is still one whole sky with no frame drawn. New clouds gather as they drift in from beyond the region's upwind edge and fade away after drifting out downwind; Edge feather sets that distance (30% of the region's shorter side by default). Where the region meets the edge of the picture (as it does for the whole frame by default), clouds drift out whole before they start to fade, and new ones are fully formed before they drift in. After the region moves, clouds that can no longer drift into it fade out and new ones form inside it. The reflection check shows what a visitor sees while walking around the puddle.

Projection settings layout: playback stays on top; the sky preview sits left, live parameters (style, audience, atmosphere, clouds, wind, rain) on the right, and tabs below the preview hold reflection check, timing, presets, camera, projection mapping and audio/log. On desktop windows at least 560 px tall the page does not scroll; panels scroll inside, so the preview stays visible while adjusting.

The canopy underside shows the same live sky as added light, with clouds only in the cloud region. "Fill" covers the whole canopy; circle and square outputs cover a centred square as wide as the canopy. Projector model, position and throw distance are undecided and not modelled.

## Camera visitor counting

The Camera tab lists every camera as soon as it opens, including wired USB webcams; the list refreshes when one is plugged in or out, and shows device names once camera access is allowed. The choice is remembered on this computer. Detection uses the YOLOX-tiny person model (trained by OpenMMLab on COCO and Human-Art; standing people are found from the front, back and side), run by ONNX Runtime Web in a background thread at about 0.2 s a frame on this M1 Pro, so the sky keeps its pace; the default minimum confidence is 50%. Detection runs once a second (timed in the background, so a hidden control window does not slow it); the count is the median of the detections over the count confirmation time (1–10 s, 3 s by default), so a person missed or a false detection in single frames never flashes a cloud, and a change counts once it holds for about half that time. On the same set of photos from the web (backs, sides and crowds) it finds more people than the previous COCO-SSD (five people seen from behind: 4 before, 5 now; a Louvre crowd: 2 before, 9 now). People in paintings or posters may also be counted; exclude them with Detection area. Only the count is read; no images are kept.

## Projection mapping

With the projector off to one side and below, casting upwards at an angle, a four-corner mapping (like a corner pin in TouchDesigner) puts the image on the canopy undistorted and edge to edge:

1. Open the projection window, move it to the projector's extended display and double-click for full screen (Esc leaves it). The projection window shows no text, buttons or pointer at all, only the sky.
2. Click Projection mapping at the top of the control page (or Start calibrating in the Projection mapping tab under the preview). A test grid and four coloured dots appear on the canopy: 1 red, 2 yellow, 3 green, 4 blue (1 = top left), with no text.
3. In the mapping panel on the control page the box stands for the projector image: drag corners 1–4 until the dots on the canopy sit on its corners; select a corner and nudge it with the arrow keys by 1 projector pixel (Shift: 10). The grid squares should look square and the circle round. To make sure no edge is left dark, drag the corners slightly past the canopy.
4. Click Finish calibrating and the grid goes. Reset corners returns to the centred starting box; Switch mapping off goes back to filling the window. The corners save automatically.

The sky is drawn at the canopy's proportions and placed on the four corners with one perspective transform (homography); for a flat canopy that removes exactly the keystone and perspective distortion of an angled projector, so the clouds keep their shape on the canopy and no separately "pre-distorted" clouds are needed. The picture's proportions must match the canopy: enter its real width and depth in the mapping panel (kept in step with the canopy study), equal for a square canopy, and use the "Fill" sky shape; with the wrong proportions the clouds are stretched. The corners are kept in `~/Documents/Ephemeral User Data/mapping.json` (the previous copy in `mapping.previous.json`), apart from parameter presets, so they survive restarts. Only the shape on a flat surface is corrected; a sagging canopy, focus and the brightness fall-off across it are not. Outside the quad the projection window is black, so nothing lights up beside the canopy.

## Simulation scope

- The water is an irregular patch of still water in the middle of the gravel (the shape is indicative), with stones above the surface all round; water-level drop, dripping and flow are not simulated. The canopy study assumes perfectly still water, a horizontal canopy and a single-point eye, and ignores heads and bodies blocking the view.
- Assumed: timber frame about 60 mm high with 30 mm boards, pavers about 200 × 100 × 50 mm in two rows, water surface about 50 mm above the floor, gravel outside the water about 8 mm above the surface, canopy at 2.27 m (previous default).
- Reflection brightness, water colour and pebble form are visual approximations without optical or material calibration.

## Presets

Under the preview, Presets → "Save current parameters" stores sky, timing, visitor count, canopy height and other manual settings. "Set as startup default" restores them on reopening. Cloud looks are saved separately under Cloud shape and texture. Once a cloud look is saved the preset file moves to version 3; earlier app versions refuse to read it and leave the file untouched, so no look is lost.

Presets live outside the app in `~/Documents/Ephemeral User Data/presets.json`, with `presets.previous.json` kept on every save. Projection mapping corners are not part of parameter presets; they are kept in `mapping.json` in the same folder. Older presets still load: sky and timing settings apply; legacy build parts and puddle fields stay in the file but are not shown. Which loop parts are on, their durations and transitions, the colours by part, style and grade strength, moonlight and the cloud appear / disappear times are saved with parameter presets too; 10.24.5 can read these presets (it uses the fields it knows). JSON export and merge import are available. `python3 launch.py --data-dir PATH` selects another folder; tests must use a temporary folder.
