Morning → day → sunset glow → starry night loops, 60 s each by default. The sun follows its real October path over Melbourne and each part takes one stretch of it: morning around sunrise, day from mid-morning to mid-afternoon, sunset glow around sunset, night a starry sky with moonlit clouds. A transition starts a set number of seconds before a part ends and ends a set number of seconds into the next part; when the next part follows on along the sun's path the sun moves on smoothly, and when a part in between is off the two skies cross-fade. Untick a part to switch it off (at least one stays on); ticking applies at once, while durations and transitions apply with Apply timing and replay. Quick, natural and slow set durations and transitions only, not which parts are on. Cloud drift and shape-change speeds are set under Wind and motion and stay constant throughout.

· Saves visuals, geometry, timing and preview count. Live wind and device permissions are not stored.

Processed on this device

Using the preview visitor count.

Detection runs once a second. The count is the median of the detections over the confirmation time, so a person missed or a false detection in single frames never flashes a cloud; a change counts once it holds for about half that time, and the clouds then fade over the cloud appear / disappear times. Only the count is used; cloud positions do not represent visitor locations. Detection uses the YOLOX-tiny person model (front, back and side views) in a background thread; people in paintings or posters may also be counted, so exclude them with Detection area. A plugged-in USB webcam appears in the list automatically.

The box stands for the projector image. First move the projection window to the projector screen and double-click for full screen, then click Start calibrating: a grid and four coloured dots (1 red, 2 yellow, 3 green, 4 blue) appear on the canopy, matching corners 1–4 in the box. Drag each corner, or select it and nudge with the arrow keys (1 projector pixel, Shift 10), until the dots sit on the canopy corners, the grid squares look square and the circle round. This step undoes the keystone of the angled projector: the picture is drawn at the canopy's proportions and then warped in perspective onto the four corners, so the clouds on the canopy keep their shape. Enter the canopy's real width and depth (equal for a square canopy) or the picture is stretched. The corners save automatically and survive restarts.

Use an extended display; the projection window follows this page's weather and style. For an angled projector, line up the four corners with the canopy corners in the Projection mapping tab. The projection window shows no text, buttons or pointer: double-click for full screen, Esc to leave it. All clouds are procedural; no artwork from the references is reused.

    Visual styleSTYLE

    Atmospheric scattering from the sun position; particle clouds lit by sunlight, skylight and city glow at night.

    Audience and imageAUDIENCE
    visitors → 4 clouds

    One cloud per visitor: it fades in on a clear patch of the canopy, drifts with the wind and leaves the picture whole; as it starts to leave, a new cloud with a different shape drifts in from the opposite (upwind) side. It fades out after they leave. In still air a cloud dissipates where it is after a minute or two and a new one forms; forming and dissipating also follow the two times below.

    At 0° north is at the top of the picture and east on the left (seen from below). In the gallery, turn it until the projected sky matches the real directions: brighter in the east in the morning, the sunset glow in the west, the midday sun to the north.

    Cloud regionREGION

    Big clouds form only inside this region (set as shares of the frame), smaller and closer together to suit its size; the sky and its thin background cloud outside it stay as they are, so the image is still one whole sky with no frame drawn. New clouds gather as they drift in from beyond the upwind edge and fade away after drifting out downwind; Edge feather sets that distance. Where the region meets the edge of the picture, clouds drift out whole before fading. Saved with parameter presets; the projection window and the 3D canopy follow it.

    Rain soundSOUND

    Rain density follows visitor count. The loop no longer has a rainy night, so rain plays only in previews.

    Atmosphere and gradeSKY

    Atmosphere changes the air itself and colour is still computed by scattering; choosing one moves turbidity to its typical value. The grade sits on top of the final image and combines with any atmosphere; strength 0 leaves the image ungraded. The night is a clear starry sky, with a full moon opposite the sun lighting the clouds.

    Colour by partCOLOUR

    Pick a part, then change it: the sky, the lit side of the clouds (sun or moon) and their shaded side (skylight and city glow) each move towards the chosen colour at the same brightness; strength 0 keeps the physically computed colour, and picking a colour sets the strength to 50%. Transparency lets the sky show through the clouds. During a transition the two parts' settings blend smoothly. Stars appear only at night. Saved with parameter presets; the projection window follows.

    Wind and motionWIND

    Reading Melbourne live wind…

    Clouds drift and change shape at steady speeds that do not vary with the weather phase or the live wind speed; live wind only sets the direction, and the clouds turn over about half a minute when it changes. Wind direction is where it blows from, converted for a canopy seen from below (north up, east left), and it turns with the sun under Canopy orientation. 1× drift crosses the image's shorter side in about 100 s. When off or offline, the manual direction applies. Data: Open-Meteo current wind, refreshed every 10 minutes.

    Simulation notesNOTES

    The pond is the whole central gravel area filled with water, drawn as a rectangle at a fixed level; individual gravel stones are not modelled.

    The canopy underside shows the same live sky as Projection settings, drawn as added light. Projector model, position and throw distance are undecided and not modelled.

    The canopy study uses plane-mirror reflection: the water is perfectly still, the canopy is a horizontal plane, the eye is a single point, and heads or bodies blocking the view are ignored. Reflection brightness and water colour are visual approximations without optical calibration. The lighting switch compares how ambient light affects projection legibility.
